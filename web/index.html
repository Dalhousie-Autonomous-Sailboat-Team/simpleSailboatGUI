        Settings

        Connection settings apply at once and last until Windbound stops.

        Serial port

        The controller prints status lines and relays XBee telemetry over its ST-Link virtual COM port. Baud must match its debug UART, or the log fills with garbled text.

        Auto-connect
        Open an ST-Link's port as soon as it is plugged in. Boards already plugged in at launch are left alone.
        Ports are scanned every second.

        Boat simulator

        The firmware-in-the-loop simulator in windbound-firmware/sim speaks the controller's serial protocol on a local socket. Start it, then choose Simulator from the connection menu.

        Addresssocket://127.0.0.1:5760

        Commanded sail

        The sail is a continuous-rotation drive, so the stick sets a direction, not an angle. The commanded boat turns its sail at this rate while the stick is out of the dead band, starting from the angle the boat reports.

        Slew rate—
        Stick dead band—
        Set in station.py (SAIL_ROTATION_RATE_DPS, SAIL_CMD_DEADBAND), shared with the desktop app.

        Depth limits

        Route legs are sampled every metre against the lake's depth grid and coloured by these limits.

        Boat draft—
        Safe depth—
        Set in route.py (BOAT_DRAFT_M, SAFE_DEPTH_M), shared with the desktop app.

        Depth grids

        Prepared lakes in lake_depth_maps/. The grids come from historical inventory maps and are approximate.

        Offline tiles

        Map tiles are kept as you view them and shown again when there's no signal, so look over the lake once at home before you go. The desktop app uses the same cache.

        Saved tiles—
        On disk—
        Stored in map_downloads/tiles.db

        Map style

        Auto shows OpenStreetMap muted to the light or dark theme. None leaves only the depth contours.

        OpenStreetMap's usage policy doesn't allow saving an area in bulk, so tiles are saved only as you view them.

        Theme

        Light reads better in direct sun at the lake; dark is easier on the eyes for long sessions.
